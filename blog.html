Thoughts on Ezekiel 1

February 24, 2025

First off, wow. The imagery here is fascinating actually. To someone who has no biblical background, I'm sure this is one of the weirdest passages in the Bible. But actually if you examine it carefully it makes sense... Though I feel very inadequate to explain everything here, so I won't try. Also I have to give credit to the Bible Project video and OT Lit. class for some of this, though it's all my own ideas.

In verses 1 through 3, Ezekiel sets the stage. He is living in exile in Babylon, and this is the year that he would have become a priest, so he's probably depressed, maybe moping by the river even. I feel like God is smirking here... He flips the tables on Ezekiel by making him a prophet instead of a priest, showing him things that the high priest himself wouldn't have seen.

In verses 4 through 21, Ezekiel describes seeing God's throne-chariot. It's alive, made up of four living creatures, each with four wings and four faces, and four wheels, each literally tall and awe-inspiring and covered in eyes. If God's chariot itself is alive, what does that say about God? He himself is alive and he is the Giver of life.

I don't exactly know what to make of this, but the four living creatures' faces correspond I think to the four faces on the banners that represented the four quarters of the camp of Israelites in Exodus. I think they are also mentioned in Revelation.

In verses 4 through 28, during this whole description, Ezekiel uses a preponderance of words pertaining to light. There is so ridiculously much light here. Lightning, fire, brightness, flashing, gleaming, shining, rainbow. And he uses these words over and over, describing the chariot, but then describing God's glory. And he layers the words too. He's trying to get across the fact that God's glory is really, really, really bright. "Our God is a consuming fire."

In verses 22 through 28, Ezekiel describes God's throne and his glory. The interesting thing about this section, is that he repeatedly uses phrases that convey that he cannot completely describe what he is seeing. For instance, "Over the heads of the living creatures there was a likeness of an expanse, shining like awe-inspiring crystal, spread out over their heads." And that's just the throne. When he gets to describing God's glory itself, it's exaggerated even more: "And above the expanse over their heads there was the likeness of a throne, in appearance like sapphire; and seated above the likeness of a throne was a likeness with a human appearance." He describes it and then finally he just says, "Such was the appearance of the likeness of the glory of God." It's like God's glory was so indescribable that he couldn't describe it, or even something like it. He had to describe something like what it looked like. He describes the chariot at length, 21 verses, but then only manages two and a half verses describing God because that's all he can muster. God is just that indescribable. Really, I think that's a good description of his holiness. He is completely set apart, completely beyond anything we can imagine. Everything we read or think can only amount to an analogy of an analogy.

In verse 28 there's something really interesting. It describes God as wearing a tunic of rainbows. With all the judgment that God pronounces in this book, all the punishment and destruction He promises, He includes this poignant reminder of his promise that He will never completely destroy the earth. He will judge it, but He promises to always leave a remnant. It's almost like God is dressing himself in his mercy.

I'm sure there's a lot more in this passage that I'm missing. But what I have found is amazing! If you have any comments, feel free to contact me.
